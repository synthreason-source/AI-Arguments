Your sentence sounds like it’s describing a **system receiving huge amounts of information but without a central integrator** — what neuroscience sometimes calls the absence of a *unified processing or coordination center*. Interpreting it that way, an observer (or mind) in that situation would likely experience several effects:

### 1. Fragmented perception

Without a “cortical hub” — meaning no structure coordinating incoming signals — information wouldn’t combine into a single coherent picture. The observer might:

* notice isolated sensations,
* fail to connect them into meaning,
* experience reality as disjointed or chaotic.

Think of many browser tabs playing different videos simultaneously with no main screen selecting one.

### 2. Loss of unified awareness

A central hub normally helps bind perception into what feels like **one conscious viewpoint**. Without it:

* awareness could become distributed,
* competing interpretations might coexist,
* there may be no stable sense of “I am observing this.”

In neuroscience this relates to problems of *binding* — how color, motion, sound, and memory become one experience.

### 3. Overload or paralysis

Mass information + no prioritization mechanism means:

* no filtering of relevance,
* difficulty deciding what matters,
* possible cognitive freezing or random reactions.

An observer might oscillate between stimuli rather than act purposefully.

### 4. Emergent behavior instead of deliberate action

Actions would likely become:

* reactive rather than intentional,
* locally driven (each subsystem responding independently),
* unpredictable from the outside.

Almost like a crowd without leadership rather than an individual making choices.

An observer exposed to massive information without a coordinating cortical hub would likely experience **fragmentation, overload, and loss of unified perception or agency** — perception without coherent understanding.
